# Current research question

Updated 20 September 2026

**Which forms of earlier state support leave capabilities that new entrants and downstream firms can use, and which mainly preserve advantages for established firms?**

The active pilot examines Chinese policy documents around WTO accession. A separate cross-country exploration finds positive associations between pre-democratization capacity and subsequent twenty-year growth in selected samples. It does not identify the sector mechanism or a necessary order of political development.

## What would change the interpretation?

- Gains extending to new entrants or downstream users, and remaining after an original preference ends, would be consistent with widely usable capabilities. Demand, technology, and selection explanations still need examination.
- Benefits confined to earlier recipients alongside continuing access barriers would be consistent with incumbent advantages. Concentration alone is insufficient because it may reflect efficiency or scale.
- Both patterns may coexist. The design must permit mixed mechanisms and inconclusive findings.

## Measurement commitments

Keep current scores identifiable as the earlier rubric version. New beneficiary and access fields are not yet validated. Do not infer capacity from later growth or describe outcome-aware revisions as preregistered. Record formal eligibility separately from actual availability and use.

## Evidence boundaries

- [Cross-country findings](../research/2026-09-20/README.md): real-data exploratory associations.
- Policy passages: source-traceable coding with uneven source strength; independent-coder validation remains incomplete.
- Saved patent counts: internally checked; upstream extraction still needs audit.
- Original event-study outputs: synthetic examples.
- Causal sector effects and institutional-sequencing effects: not identified.
