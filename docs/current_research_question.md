# Current questions and evidence boundaries

Updated 25 September 2026 with a bounded documentary pilot; the broader questions and chronology remain unchanged.

## Organizing question

**How do inherited state institutions and policy support shape later development, and who can use the capabilities they leave behind?**

The project began in late March 2026 according to the author's retrospective account. The note dated April already distinguished durable capacity-building from incumbent entrenchment. September sharpened the measurement requirements; it did not originate that distinction. See [history and evidence](project_history.md).

## Keep these comparisons separate

| Question | Unit and comparison | Current evidence | What is not identified |
|---|---|---|---|
| Does prior capacity relate to growth after democratization? | Countries with qualifying observed transitions; different pre-transition capacities | September exploratory regressions | Necessary political sequence; effect of authoritarian rule; what earlier democracy would have changed |
| Which inherited policy resources are accessible beyond original recipients? | Policy instruments, eligible users and firms; the China WTO pilot is one candidate setting | Formal policy coding and saved outcome series requiring audit | Actual access, beneficiary effects, or a causal effect of earlier support |

**The mechanism question:** Which forms of earlier state support leave capabilities that new entrants and downstream firms can use, and which mainly preserve advantages for established firms?

This mechanism existed in the Korea proposal. Moving it to trade opening changes the event, feasible comparisons and counterfactual. Neither branch supplies missing identification for the other.

## What could change the interpretation?

- Documented use by new or downstream firms, with benefits persisting after an original preference ends, would be consistent with broadly usable capabilities. Other changes still need examination.
- Documented access restrictions and benefits concentrated among existing recipients would support an incumbent-advantage interpretation. Concentration alone is insufficient because efficiency or scale may also contribute.
- Both mechanisms can operate together. Incomplete evidence can leave the question unresolved.

## Current empirical pilot — 25 September 2026

**What evidence distinguishes state-supported automotive testing infrastructure from formal arrangements that list multiple testing institutions at the same venue? What further evidence would establish access by manufacturers?**

A 2005 notice supplies 12 institution records and 23 links to four venue names. It documents a specific administrative arrangement after WTO accession. It does not measure transactions, new entrant eligibility, affordability, or policy impact. Earlier histories require separating physical construction from accreditation; retrospective accounts are not original project approvals.

[Findings, sources, data and reproduction](../research/2026-09-25/README.md). This pilot follows an existing coded passage; it does not resolve the broad sequencing hypothesis or commit the project to a causal WTO design.
