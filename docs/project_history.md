# Project history and evidence for dates

Recorded retrospectively on 20 September 2026. This page distinguishes when the research began, dates printed on documents, and dates when files entered GitHub. They are different kinds of evidence.

## Origin

I began this project in **late March 2026**, when I formed an initial hypothesis about state building, democratization, and long-run prosperity and put forward a research proposal. This starting point is based on my retrospective account, added to the repository in September. The original March version has not been located in the materials reviewed for this update. The exact day, format of the first proposal, and any circulation or discussion dates are not established here.

My initial conjecture was that, in some Asian development experiences, state capacity built before democratization might help explain later prosperity. The stronger claim that such a political sequence is necessary remains unproven. The project must allow evidence against it.

## What continued, and what changed

The distinction between **durable capabilities** and **incumbent advantages was already in the note dated April**. It should not be presented as a new idea introduced in September. September clarified its measurement requirements and the limits of existing evidence.

The move from Korea's democratization to China's WTO accession changed the setting and the political or economic event under study. The two events cannot be treated as equivalent. The policy-text pilot can develop measurement of inherited support, but cannot by itself establish a necessary sequence of state building and democratization.

The September cross-country regressions are another, more aggregate comparison. They examine associations among observed democratic transitions. Their findings neither validate the policy index nor establish the sector-level mechanisms.

## Research status and attribution

This is independent research with substantial AI assistance and no formal research supervisor at present. The question and motivation are mine. Documents, coding, calculations and interpretation remain subject to revision and external review. An informal conversation, a named prospective mentor, or a document marked accepted is not evidence of formal supervision or independent validation.

New evidence about early dates should be added as a dated correction. Git history will not be backdated, and missing stages will not be reconstructed as if they were contemporaneously documented.
